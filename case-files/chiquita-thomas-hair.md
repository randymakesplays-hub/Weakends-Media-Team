## Diagnosis

**What's actually working here:** the spec-build broke a four-week silence. Randy sent a
finished site with no ask attached and she replied the same morning, warmly, for the first
time since Aug 7. That is the single highest-leverage move in this entire thread and it is
worth generalizing — *build first, then ask* beat four weeks of check-in texts.

**What she is:** the intake doc's read holds — weak social presence, no website, **$0
marketing spend**, all sales hand-to-hand. She is not a business with a marketing problem;
she is a business that does not yet exist online. Everything has to be built, which is why
the startup package was the right call.

**The Parfait detail is the real find.** She came out of an established wig brand. That
means category knowledge, probable supplier relationships, and a personal network of
customers — the reason hand-to-hand selling works for her at all. It also means she has a
reference point for what a "real" brand looks like, which is exactly what Randy is
leaning on with the *"bigger brand than Parfait"* question. Good instinct, wrong moment
(see Risk #2).

## ⚠️ Risk #1 — the $500 was withdrawn and replaced with nothing

This is the most consequential thing in the file.

> *"Tbh I think me charging you $500 for a website would be a disservice… I think in the
> beginning if we worked out a % that makes sense, I can help you secure the consistent
> revenue you're looking for."*

A **priced, agreed-shaped offer was voluntarily removed** and replaced with:

- no percentage number
- no definition of what the % applies to
- no floor, no minimum, no term
- no trigger for when it starts
- delivered over text, with no call, to a prospect whose reply latency runs to weeks

$500 from a $0-marketing-spend seller was real, collectable money for work **already
delivered** — the site exists. The rev-share may well be the better long-term structure,
but the move as executed converts a closeable invoice into an open-ended negotiation.

**If she says "sure, what percent?" there is currently no answer prepared.** Decide the
number before she replies, not after.

**Percent of what, specifically:** hair e-commerce carries real COGS. A % of *gross
revenue* can exceed her *net margin* on a unit and make her worse off per sale — which
kills the deal on her first month's math. A % of *profit* requires visibility into her
books she has no reason to grant a new vendor. The workable version is usually a % of
**attributable new revenue** (orders through the site/ads), with a monthly floor.

## Other risks

2. **Two asks in one message.** The % proposal and the Parfait question went out back to
   back at 9:54 AM. The emotional question is the louder one, so she will likely answer
   that and skip the pricing — leaving the commercial term unresolved while the
   conversation feels like it progressed.
3. **No attribution infrastructure exists.** A revenue share requires tracking which sales
   came from the work. She has no analytics, no CRM, no tracked checkout, and sells
   hand-to-hand. **A rev-share is unenforceable and unverifiable until that is built** —
   which makes the tracking layer a prerequisite deliverable, not an upsell.
4. **The site is on `up.railway.app`.** That is a preview/staging host, not a brand asset.
   No custom domain has been bought, and a wig brand on a `railway.app` subdomain reads as
   unfinished to her customers. Domain + DNS + custom email is the next concrete step.
5. **Six weeks, no revenue, no signature.** Aug 7 to Sep 16 with a four-week dead zone.
   She is warm but slow. Every additional undefined variable extends the cycle.
6. **Thread lives in personal iMessage**, not GHL. Nothing here is in the CRM, so nothing
   is visible to the pipeline, tagged, or recoverable if the phone is lost. Get it in.
7. **Sep 14 call is unrecorded.** The pain points Randy is pricing against exist only in
   his memory. For an account where the commercial structure is being rebuilt around
   "hearing your exact pain points," that is a thin foundation.
8. **She asked for email. Everything still goes to iMessage.**
   Aug 6: *"Yes, please feel free to send it to my email!"* Two days later the website
   inspiration went out over iMessage — and got no reply. Every unanswered message in this
   thread (Aug 7 invite check, Aug 9 inspiration, and now the Sep 16 pricing text) is in
   the channel she did not choose. **Her email address does not appear anywhere in the
   thread.** Get it, and put the pricing conversation where she said she reads things.
9. **Two no-shows before the spec build, not one.** Aug 6 she missed the 12:00 outright
   (*"I promise I will make it"*); Aug 7's rebook never happened either. Her stated intent
   is consistently good and her follow-through is consistently poor — which is precisely
   why **an undefined % she has to think about is the wrong instrument for this account.**
   She converts on concrete things handed to her (the finished site), not on decisions she
   has to initiate.

## Transferable lesson

**The spec-build works.** Four weeks of silence, one unrequested finished website, same-day
warm reply. Consider making it the standard second touch on any lead that goes dark after
showing initial interest — it costs build time and converts dead threads.

**The pricing pattern does not.** Across Pete, Alec (Flawless), Jose, Aaron and now
Chiquita, the same gap recurs: real work committed or delivered, **no agreed terms**. With
Chiquita it is sharpest because a price existed and was given back. Whatever the eventual
model, the agency needs a default: a rate card, a standard rev-share %, and a floor — so
the terms conversation is a quote, not an invention, every time.
