## What happened on the call

### Call 1 — Randy gets punched in the mouth, and recovers

Randy opened on the standard hook. Alec killed it in one sentence:

> *"Yeah, man, it's under repair at the moment, and I'm not interested in buying another one at the moment."*

Randy's response is the best opening save in the portfolio: *"Well, man, hey, I guess you already kind of punched me in the mouth there."* No push, no rebuttal. Alec immediately warmed up and explained himself unprompted:

> *"I get phone calls constantly about it. I went through godaddy.com, built a website, and I don't know if it was hacked or whatever, but the website I can no longer use, and right now they're saying that it's, quote-unquote, 'under repair' and they're not charging me for it until then."*

And then the thing that should have set the agenda for the whole account:

> *"It's been one of those things where whenever people say, 'Hey, look, I was trying to look at your website,' da, da, da, da, da. I tell them just to give me a call. They give me a call, and we end up working something out. It's usually easier 'cause some of the things that I had posted on there they weren't able to understand."*

Randy went to the free-website reframe — *"I'm not looking to just kind of sell you a website. I actually do websites for free"* — and landed a genuinely good line: *"if you're the one doing the detailing on the car plus you're trying to play around with GoDaddy and be like a tech engineer, that sounds like a waste of time."* Alec agreed twice.

Randy also described himself: *"I have a very small agency. I actually help small business owners kind of just manage their systems."* And he floated the only compensation framing that appears anywhere in call 1 — vague, and almost certainly the thing Alec was referring back to on call 2:

> *"if I can come in and help you, you know, kill, like, couple birds with one stone, hey, maybe I can get a piece of the pie at the end of the rainbow."*

Then Randy used an unnamed detailing client as the proof point (**he never names the client on either call** — the identification as Carolina Gloss is inference from the research file, not from the transcript):

> *"right now I have a client, you know, he actually does detailing as well. And, you know, I built his whole website. I'm running all his ads. I'm managing all his leads. So I don't know if that's something that sounds sexy to you, but, um, you know, it doesn't sound too sexy to be playing around with GoDaddy trying to fix your website."*

## Diagnosis

**This is the portfolio's clearest demand-constrained lead.** (Chiquita and Spotless are also classed demand-constrained in `case-files/README.md` — Alec is the sharpest case, not the only one.)

The ICP test across this book is: does the business need more demand, or more supply? Pete Rushford is turning work away and needs technicians (`case-files/pete-rushford-in-home-services/README.md`). Alec is the inverse:

- He **named ads as his priority twice, in his own words**, and both times put the website below it — first *"ads… Not a website,"* then, of the free site, *"That's, I mean, whatever."*
- He is executing a **deliberate service-mix pivot** — out of detailing, into PPF/ceramic/tint/wrap — and says the protection side *"bring[s] in a different type of clientele."* He already lands PPF work (a $2,000 job the next day); what he does not have is **volume** of it. The gap is demand for the higher-ticket line, not capability.
- **He has open capacity.** Friday is empty. Thursday is "a couple details" and might free up. An operator with an empty Friday and a stated desire for bigger jobs is the textbook paid-acquisition candidate.
- **The unit economics support paid traffic better than anything else in this book.**

**The margin math, stated carefully.** One job: **$2,000 revenue, "less than $200 worth of material, if that."** That is **≥90% material margin, ~$1,800+ contribution gross of labor.** Caveats that matter: this is one self-reported job, the vehicle and coverage level were never stated, the job duration was never stated, and labor is clearly substantial (an hour-plus of pre-weeding the night before, plus install day). Still — against the brief's own $25–50 CPL benchmark, even a 10% lead-to-job close rate puts ~$250–500 of ad spend against ~$1,800 of contribution. **That is a business that can afford to buy customers.** It is the strongest paid-acquisition case in the portfolio and it should be the spine of Friday's pitch.

**The counter-signal, honestly stated.** He is not starving. His phone funnel works:

> *"I tell them just to give me a call. They give me a call, and we end up working something out."*

He has run **months with no website** (~nine, if December means December 2025) and is still booked Wednesday and Thursday. He is demand-constrained *relative to where he wants to go*, not relative to survival. That distinction sets the urgency ceiling and it is why "I'll build you a free website" is not the lever — the free website solves a problem he has already routed around. **The lever is: you cannot pivot to $2,000 PPF jobs on a referral funnel built by 21 years of detailing customers, because those are the customers you're trying to stop serving.**

**He is a burned buyer — three failed tech engagements deep.**

1. GoDaddy site: dead since ~December, locked out of the account, whoever said they'd fix it *"never got back to it."*
2. AI chatbot: *"worked for probably three days, and then it failed"* — and he **thinks** it crashed his site (*"I think the main reason why my… website crashed"* — his own words are a guess, not a diagnosis).
3. A prior vendor who *"couldn't log into the, the meta whatever. Like, something was blocking it."*

Two of the three (#1 and #3) are **access/recovery** failures rather than strategy failures — precisely the category of work Randy has now spoken for sight unseen. #2 is a functionality failure and is undiagnosed by anyone.

**His arithmetic checks out.** 28 minus 7 equals 21. He is internally consistent on age and tenure, which is worth noting because he is not on the "couple months" vs "December" website question.

## Risks — ranked

### 1. No price, no rate, no terms — and this time the prospect asked first
The most consequential item in the file. Alec explicitly asked *"when I start making money or the website starts bringing me… in revenue, I pay you?"* and Randy answered *"Don't even worry for that."* Randy has now committed, unpriced: a full website rebuild from scratch and retrieval of the domain. He has additionally *claimed* Meta recovery capability (*"I deal with meta troubleshoots all the time"*) and described Facebook ad account setup and lead-flow integration as what he'd do — those are scoped but not yet promised in the same way the website was. The only price-shaped thing ever said on either call is Randy's call-1 *"maybe I can get a piece of the pie at the end of the rainbow."*

**Failure scenario:** Randy builds the site before Friday. Friday's call opens with a deliverable already handed over for free. Alec — 21 years in trade, 28, sharp, already burned by three vendors — has no reason to convert a free thing into a paid thing, and the one moment he was *volunteering* to discuss money has passed. The ads work, the only thing he actually wants, then gets negotiated from a position where Randy has already given away his leverage.

**This is the third account with the identical pattern.** Pete: real recruiting labor, no fee (`case-files/pete-rushford-in-home-services/README.md`, Risk #1). Chiquita: a $500 price voluntarily withdrawn and replaced with an undefined percentage (`case-files/chiquita-thomas-hair.md`, Risk #1). Alec: a buying question declined outright. **The agency needs a default rate card and a standard rev-share floor so the terms conversation is a quote, not an invention, every time.** Decide Alec's number before 11:00 Friday.

### 2. The meeting time is not actually agreed
Randy offered *"11:00 AM your time"* **before** asking where Alec was. Alec then said Arizona, and *"about two hours behind you."* Arizona does not observe DST — in September it is MST, UTC-7. Central Daylight is UTC-5, so the two-hour gap is only correct **if Randy is on Central Time.** Eastern is three hours. **Randy never stated his own location or timezone on either call**, and his personal number is a 704 (Charlotte) while his business number is a 346 (Houston). The only timezone arithmetic performed on the call was done by the prospect, about the salesperson's location, which the salesperson never confirmed.

The booking record reads **"Fri Sep 18, 11:00" with no timezone at all** — and the portfolio index (`case-files/README.md`) has already resolved it, wrongly, to **"Fri Sep 18, 11:00 CT."** Nobody agreed to Central. Alec agreed to 11:00 *his* time, in Arizona. Fix the README too.
