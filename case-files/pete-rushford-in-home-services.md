## What actually happened on the call

Randy opened on the standard hook (no website, Google traffic can't convert). Pete
rejected it cold and kept rejecting it — **five separate times** — through free website
offers, the "you're doing yourself a disservice" reframe, and the CRM/automation pitch.

Every pitch died because the premise was wrong. Randy was selling demand. Pete has too
much demand.

The call turned at ~9 minutes when Pete said the real thing:

> "If you had good reliable people, I'd be happy to send you work... I need technicians
> that can actually do what the hell they say they do."

Randy dropped the website pitch entirely and took the recruiting job. That was the
correct read and it's the only reason this lead is alive.

## Diagnosis

**Pete's business:**
- Solo operator, late 50s, licensed + bonded, 5-star, 20+ years
- Bread and butter: rental-property make-readies and maintenance for absentee landlords
  (China, AZ, CA, NY, PA) — never met most of them, they don't ask price, only "how fast"
- ~10–15 inbound calls/day, ~$1,000/day revenue on 2–4 daily call-outs
- Already has paint crews + roofing crews on call
- Sitting on 3 unanswered jobs from yesterday. Turning away / giving away work
- Stepped away from remodels (time-consuming, worse payout than call-outs)
- One $500k concrete job runs through his license
- Invoicing: a $120/yr app that syncs to QuickBooks and feeds his taxes, thousands of
  customers logged. **He will not migrate.** Do not attack this.

**The actual bottleneck:** manpower. He's burned through ~50 people. Cannot find
reliable general handymen. That is the ceiling on his revenue, not leads.

**What he needs in a sub:**
- General handyman scope: outlets, light switches, dishwashers, drains, garbage
  disposals — simple, quick, high-margin call-outs
- Own general tools, own vehicle, work ethic, common sense
- Treat the customer "like they're their mother"
- Liability insurance (case-by-case if he knows them); working as independent contractors
- **Ideal profile:** already-independent contractors with their own thing going who want
  overflow to fill gaps — not employees, not people dependent on him
- Pay reference: his past subs took home **$400–500/day** on mostly indoor work

**His guarantee model:** if a sub's work has a problem, the customer calls Pete, Pete
goes out personally. That's why he's picky. That's why 50 people failed.

## Risks — read before Friday

1. **No commercial terms exist.** Randy has committed real recruiting labor with no
   price, no rate, no per-placement fee, no contract. "It comes full circle" is not a
   deal. Pete is a 20-year operator who will happily take free subs. **Lock the
   structure before delivering the portfolio** — per-placement fee, or a % of the
   overflow revenue, or a monthly ops-manager retainer.
2. **Friday deadline is self-imposed and hard.** Randy said Friday on the call and "5
   solid screenings" over SMS. Currently 1 (Jose). Missing it kills credibility with the
   exact type of guy who has heard weekly pitches for 20 years.
3. **Screening quality is the whole product.** 50 people have already failed Pete. A
   weak candidate is worse than no candidate — one bad sub means Pete drives out
   personally and eats it. Screen hard against his checklist: tools, vehicle, insurance,
   already-independent, customer manner.
4. **Do not re-pitch the website as the lead item.** Randy correctly moved it to "I don't
   mind building the backend site in the meantime." Keep it exactly there — free,
   secondary, unrequested. Leading with it again resets the call to minute one.
5. **Pete is not in the pipeline as a marketing client and shouldn't be scored as one.**
   He is a supply-side/ops account. If he converts, it's on recruiting economics.
