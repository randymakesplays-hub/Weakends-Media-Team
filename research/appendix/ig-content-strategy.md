# Appendix — Instagram Static-Content Strategy for Car Detailing

Full research findings (agent pass, Aug 2026). Condensed version in §5 of the main brief.

## 1. Grid post types (ranked by consensus)
1. **Before/after transformations** — #1 in every detailing-industry source. Highest-performing sub-types: **interior transformations** (pet hair, coffee stains, kid debris — most *shareable*: "parents and pet owners tag their friends"); **neglected daily driver → showroom** ("massive organic reach because it feels almost unbelievable"); headlight restoration (cheap, obvious contrast); decontamination reveals (iron remover turning purple = ceramic upsell education); oxidation/correction, wheels, engine bays, water spots.
2. **Close-up gloss/reflection shots** — position the car so paint reflects something clean (sky, architecture), never the photographer; golden hour or overcast; the grid's aesthetic backbone.
3. **Process/action shots** — foam cannon, polisher, kit flat lays, **the mobile rig/van** (proof you come to them). Supporting content.
4. **Review/testimonial graphics** — minimal only: "the words should do the work"; a real Google-review screenshot beats an over-designed graphic; pair with a photo of that job's car.
5. **Educational carousels** — "how often should you detail," ceramic vs wax, why automatic washes scratch. Save-magnets; fill the 70% non-promo quota.
6. **Price/package posts** — frame as outcomes: "Your paint deserves lifetime protection — here's how we do it" (not "Introducing our Diamond Package"). 3 clear tiers with transparent or "starting at" pricing "filters out price-shoppers"; ranges beat "DM for quote" (Kejoola).
7. Static 50/50 splits — fine, but the 2-slide carousel reveal is the higher-engagement version.
8. Community extras: car-of-the-month, milestones, UGC reposts.

**70/30 rule:** ≤30% promotional, ≥70% educational/proof/entertainment.

## 2. Before/after credibility protocol
- **Same angle, same distance, same framing** — different angles create doubt. Mark camera positions; shoot befores from 3–4 fixed positions (front ¾, driver door, each seat, trunk) *before touching the car*, repeat exactly after.
- **Same lighting** — overcast ideal ("the sky becomes one giant softbox"; "shows true paint clarity without harsh reflections"). Interiors: same doors open, same time of day or same portable light.
- **Lock exposure** (ISO 100–400, f/8–f/11, ≥1/125s) so the after isn't just brighter — a brightened/filtered after is the #1 staged tell.
- No filters on B/A pairs; equal light adjustments only.
- Labels: simple BEFORE/AFTER in readable type; arrows/circles for specifics ok; no overdesign.
- Formats: side-by-side L/R (mobile-easiest; doors, seats, wheels), top/bottom (hoods, full profiles), **2–3 slide carousel** (split as slide 1, full-res before + after as "receipts").
- Caption specifics: vehicle, condition, service, hours ("3 years of pet hair, 4.5 hours" > "amazing transformation!").
- Staged red flags: angle/zoom change, sun-after/shade-before, wet "after" paint (shoot DRY), moved location, heavy HDR.

## 3. Stories
- **Link sticker is the highest-leverage element** — more followers see stories than tap the profile; **rename sticker text** ("Book now", "2 slots left").
- Scarcity pattern: "slots filling fast this weekend in [Area]"; formula: "3 slots left this week — DM 'BOOK' to claim" + sticker fallback; run availability story in parallel with each feed post.
- DM keyword + sticker combo (conversation path + tap path).
- Daily cadence: spot-of-the-day, process clips, product close-ups, client reactions, booking confirmations ("another Cary driveway booked for Friday").
- Interactive: polls ("Interior or exterior worse in your car?"), sliders, quizzes ("guess how long this took"), question box (harvest FAQ carousel content), **countdown stickers on offer deadlines**.
- Review reshares: screenshot Google reviews → stories → Reviews highlight.
- Professional accounts: "Book Now" action button; DMs have native Booking Link attachment.

## 4. Highlight covers
- 1080×1080 PNG; key element inside ~750px circle, guaranteed-visible inside ~500px; test at phone size.
- One icon OR one word per cover; pick ONE system (icons / color-coded / text) and never mix. White line-icons on brand-color circles is the prescribed pattern.
- **5–6 highlights max** — they function as a mini-website nav: `Book · Before/After · Reviews · Pricing · Ceramic · FAQ` (+ optional seasonal e.g. "Pollen SZN"). ~10–11 chars visible per label.
- Keep stocked — a Reviews highlight last updated 14 months ago is anti-proof.

## 5. Carousels
- ~35% more saves than single images; highest engagement rate of any format (0.50% by followers, Metricool 2026).
- **Hook → value → CTA.** Hook slide owns 80% of the outcome: specific number/claim, payoff promise in 8–10 words, high-contrast type, swipe cue, **logo ≤15% of canvas**, no stock backgrounds.
- Value slides: one idea each, ≤25 words, progress indicator, consistent grid.
- CTA slide mirrors hook slide design ("a jarring CTA reads as 'ad'"). Archetypes: save-this, share, **comment-keyword lead capture ("Comment GLOSS for a quote")**, link-in-bio sparingly.
- Specs: 7–10 slides education / 2–3 reveals; **4:5 throughout, never mixed**; two type sizes max; one accent color.
- Detailing-specific: step-by-step process; detail-frequency by vehicle use; ceramic vs wax; seasonal tips; **FAQ carousel** (from question-sticker harvest); **"what's included" breakdowns** — one included item per slide shown being done on a real car (beats text-only menus).

## 6. Captions, CTAs, offers
- CTA early (truncation at ~125 chars); or bake CTA into the image.
- Rotate 3 caption modes: question / value / CTA — not 100% "book now."
- "Book this week and save 20%" > static pricing; direct booking links > multi-step funnels ("every extra step loses somebody").
- Bio must state location/service area; geotag everything; #RaleighDetailing / #[City]Detailer patterns.
- **Offer numbers:** first-time ~10% off; flash sales 20–30% off for 24–72h (countdown sticker); rebooking 10–15% within 7 days (intervals: wash 2–4wks, full 60–90 days, ceramic maintenance 6–12mo); referral/loyalty (free wash after 5, +67% LTV / −45% CAC per Opensend); **subscription plans 20–30% under one-off pricing — huge for mobile (recurring driveway route)**.
- **Seasonal (NC):** ~4 campaigns/year, 4–8 week windows. **Spring pollen is Carolina gold** — NC pine pollen peaks late March–early April, acidic, water-activated, etches clear coat; dry-wiping scratches. Raleigh detailers (Johnson's, Oak City, Street Legends, Echelon Elite) run whole pollen content series; sealant/ceramic = the pollen-defense upsell. Winter: salt/brine neutralization. Summer: UV, road-trip prep, bugs, leather. Holidays: gift cards. Frame as helpful reminders, not sales.

## 7. Cadence & grid aesthetics
- **3–5 feed posts/week + daily stories**; consistency beats bursts ("reliably 1–2 weekly outperforms sporadic daily").
- Model week: 1 reel, 2 photos (one B/A, one educational), 1 carousel, daily stories.
- **Batch-shoot 10–15 pieces on one busy day → 2–3 weeks of content.**
- Cohesion: one filter/edit process ("same family" look); brand color appearing in almost every post. For Carolina Gloss: cool-neutral edit w/ slightly lifted blues + Carolina-blue accent bar/label device.
- Layout patterns (checkerboard, row-by-row, middle-column) exist but rigid patterns break with one extra post — loose rhythm (every 4th–5th post a designed graphic) is more durable; use grid planning mostly for color consistency.

## 8. Real photos vs designed graphics
- **~70–80% real / 20–30% designed.** All-graphics grids underperform because: proof deficit (detailing is bought on evidence; templates carry none); ad-pattern blindness (polished template grids pattern-match franchise/spam); engagement data (real work beats graphics; testimonial screenshots beat testimonial graphics); local discovery (recognizable driveways/neighborhoods/van signal "actually operates here").
- Reserve designed graphics for: review quotes on subtle brand background, package/price breakdowns, educational carousel frames wrapped around real photos, seasonal offers. Brand cohesion via photo edit + small logo device, not templating everything.
