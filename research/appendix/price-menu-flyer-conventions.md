# Appendix — Mobile Detailing Price Menu & Service Flyer Design Conventions

Full research findings (agent pass, Aug 2026). Condensed version in §4 of the main brief.

## 1. Layout patterns

**Dominant skeleton** (QuoteIQ template tool, flyerease, real menus at Kings Mobile Detailing SC, Detail King Express PA, Panda Hub):
1. Business header (name, logo, phone/email)
2. **3–5 core packages** — QuoteIQ warns against more; "keep package names plain enough that a customer knows what they're getting without asking"
3. **Vehicle-size columns: Sedan | SUV | Truck/Van** (Halo Nashville example: full detail $245 sedan / $285 small SUV-truck / $300 large SUV-minivan)
4. Add-ons as separate line items (pet hair, ozone, engine bay, headlights, ceramic)
5. Fine-print row: service radius, condition surcharges, policies

**Interior/exterior split** — two legitimate approaches: package-axis (Express Wash & Vacuum / Interior / Exterior / Full — QuoteIQ's recommended four) or within-package side-by-side checklists (Detail King Express PA).

**"Starting at" vs fixed pricing:** Mobile Tech RX argues condition-based pricing makes ~40% more money than fixed menus (inspect first, then quote). The practical compromise on real menus: **"starting at" prices by vehicle size + condition-surcharge disclaimer** (Kings SC uses ranges "Bronze $300–350"; Panda Hub "from $79.91"). Do NOT print hard fixed prices with no condition escape hatch, and do NOT hide prices entirely (flyerease lists it as a top flyer mistake).

**Other conventions:** "Most Popular" badge on middle tier (DesignWiz); maintenance/subscription plans as separate block (Kings SC: weekly/bi-weekly/monthly $90–150/visit); ceramic/correction broken out separately ($600–2,500+ per QuoteIQ).

## 2. Typography & hierarchy
- Display: bold condensed all-caps sans — **Bebas Neue, Oswald** repeatedly named; **Saira** (motorsport-designed); DesignWiz names Futura/Roboto Condensed.
- Body: **Montserrat, Proxima Nova** class; avoid decorative scripts.
- Package names: large bold caps w/ generous letter-spacing. Prices: big, hierarchy via **size and spacing, not effects**. Inclusions: small light checklists. **1–2 typefaces total** — multiple competing typefaces is AI-design tell #1 (Lien Design).
- Emphasis: soft shadows or thin rules — never chrome bevels.

## 3. Color & material: premium vs cheap
- **Premium:** dark grounds (black/charcoal/deep navy) + silver/platinum/restrained gold; "dark charcoal with electric blue accents"; high-gloss vehicle photography with dramatic lighting is THE dominant visual — gloss lives in the photo, not chrome text; generous negative space; matte or spot-UV print finishes.
- **Cheap:** bright yellows/hot pinks/neons ("signal budget services" — neon-on-dark only for young tuner audiences); discount-first headlines; loud clip-art; everything-bold, no hierarchy.
- **Carolina Gloss specifics:** light blue = water/cleanliness coding (Auto Laundry News, ManyPixels). Premium move (KreaFolk): **light blue accent on dark ground** — "a dark background gives strong contrast that highlights bright tones like light blue." Carolina blue is soft/desaturated, not neon, so it sidesteps the trap. White-ground = "fresh/clean" car-wash coded; black-ground = "premium detail studio" coded.

## 4. Cheap/AI tells for flyers (Lien Design, Cemoh, 404 Media, Left Hand Design)
1. Garbled/warped text ("shapes that look like letters")
2. Chunky slightly-melted 3D lettering with flourishes — the most culturally-coded AI look
3. The default neon-sunset gradient glow
4. Mystery floating objects/"glowing debris"; zero white space
5. No hierarchy ("everything is loud, so nothing is loud")
6. Generic 3D-render cars: inconsistent lighting, faint shadows, subtle warps
7. Meaningless badges/shields/laurels; fake certification seals
8. Random icon confetti (sparkles, floating shapes)
9. Inconsistent brand rendering between posts (logo warping/color drift)
10. Buzzword taglines ("Elevate Your Premium Journey")

Commercial stakes (Cemoh): 63% less likely to buy from brands using AI ads; 73% trust AI ads less; 54% of Gen Z say heavy AI decreases trust.

Counter-moves: real typography set in design software (never rendered in an image); authentic photography of actual work/rig (detailing is uniquely rich here); one reusable template system; rigid palette; 1–2 fonts; deliberate white space; consistent single-source lighting in composites.

## 5. Package naming + Southeast price benchmarks

| Family | Examples | Positioning |
|---|---|---|
| Metals/gems | Bronze/Silver/Gold (Kings SC); Gold/Platinum (Detail King PA) | Classic tiering |
| Speed/depth | Express / Wash & Wax / Deep Interior / Full Detail | Function-first |
| Branded | Panda X / Revive / **Black** (Panda Hub) | App-era; "Black" as top tier is a premium convention |
| Descriptive-premium | Essential Protection / Premium Care / Concierge | Luxury positioning |
| Levels | Level 1/2/3 | Utilitarian |

QuoteIQ counterweight: plain names convert. Hybrid = plain descriptor + brand modifier (a "Gloss" family).

**Benchmarks:** Charlotte (APC): full detail $150–300; Panda Hub Charlotte: express from $79.91, full from $227, protection from $272 (claims ~30% under market). Raleigh (Mobile Detail Man): basic ~$100, full $250–400; jobs observed $79.99–291.80. Nashville (Halo): interior from $200/$215/$245; full from $245/$285/$300. SC (Kings): Bronze $300–350, Silver $450–500, Gold w/ 1-step correction $700–750. National 2026 (QuoteIQ/Jobber/Invoice Fly): express $50–130; interior $100–330; exterior $100–250; full $180–500 ($180–350 sedan / $230–500 SUV-truck). **SUV/truck premium +20–30%.** Add-ons: pet hair $40–100; ozone $50–120; engine bay $40–100; headlights ~$100/pair; shampoo from $50; 1-step correction +$100–300; ceramic $600–2,500+.

## 6. Info blocks beyond prices
"We come to you" stated explicitly (omitting it = top mistake) · named service cities/radius · per-package what's-included checklists · estimated duration per package (Detail King essential element) · booking CTA + channels + **QR to booking portal** (campaign-specific QR/landing pages for tracking) · deposit/cancellation line (Mobile Detail Man Raleigh: 25% non-refundable, forfeited <48hrs) · personal-items fine print (Detail King Express) · REAL trust badges (insured, satisfaction guarantee, review count) · IG handle + #[City]Detailer · payment methods & hours · guarantee language.

IG-specific: pricing belongs in a **permanent Pricing highlight** alongside Services / Before & After / Reviews / FAQ — "new profile visitors look at Highlights before the feed" (Undrdog/Socialmon).

## 7. Dimensions & formats

| Placement | Spec | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| IG feed | 1080×1350 (4:5) | Grid previews 3:4 → keep prices/logo centered (loses ~7% top/bottom) |
| IG feed (new) | **1080×1440 (3:4)** | Displays uncropped in feed AND grid — strong for menu posts |
| Story/Reel | 1080×1920 | Center safe zone |
| Highlight cover | 1080×1920 canvas, reads from center circle | One glyph per highlight |
| Carousel | 4:5 or 3:4 per card | One package per card + summary card |
| Google Business | 1200×900 (4:3), central 900×900 safe zone | PNG for text graphics; Google favors clean photos |
| Print | 8.5×11 poster; 5.5×8.5 / 4×6 counter card; **4×9 door hanger** (residential canvassing) | 300 DPI vector PDF; gloss or matte+spot-UV stock |
