# Carolina Gloss Detailing — Creative Research Brief

**Prepared by:** Weakends Media Team · **Date:** August 30, 2026
**Scope:** Research only — no designs produced yet. This brief feeds the upcoming static-image creative system: ad creatives, stories, highlights, grid posts, price-menu/package/FAQ flyers.
**Client mandate:** "Very high quality static images… no cheap shit that screams AI." Mix of AI-assisted imagery, real photos with swappable variables, and clean designed flyers.

---

## 0. Executive summary

1. **The client's instinct is empirically correct.** In the six Instagram references reviewed (pixels analyzed, not just captions), the two posts built on **real photos with minimal text overlay earned 712 and 840 likes**; the AI-mascot poster, AI-logo collage, and template price menus earned **1–23 likes**. Real-photo-first isn't a taste preference — it's the performance pattern.
2. **Price transparency is an open lane in this market.** Nearly every Charlotte-area competitor hides pricing behind quote forms. A clean "starting at" price menu (kept compatible with Carolina Gloss's quote-first funnel) is a genuine differentiator — but it belongs in a **Pricing highlight + pinned post + flyer**, not as recurring feed content (price-menu posts are reference material, not engagement drivers).
3. **The winning creative formula for statics:** real driveway/transformation photo → clean brand-color overlay strip (Carolina blue on near-black) → one headline + one price/offer + one CTA → stars-with-review-count trust strip → city name. Design lives in the overlay layer; the camera supplies the pixels that prove the work.
4. **AI has a narrow, safe role:** backgrounds, cleanup, and atmosphere only — never the car's badges, wheels, plates, or paint reflections, never rendered text. Anything AI-touched gets grain, desaturation, and a unified grade.
5. **The system to build next:** 6–8 locked templates with swappable real-photo zones ("the photo is the variable, everything else is constant"), a 6-highlight cover set, a 4:5 carousel frame for FAQ/education, a price-menu flyer (digital + print), and a seasonal offer calendar anchored on **NC pollen season** — the signature local campaign.

## 4. Price menu & flyer conventions (what the best ones do)

**Canonical skeleton** (QuoteIQ, flyerease, real menus in SC/PA/TN): header w/ logo+contact → **3–5 packages max** → **Sedan | SUV | Truck columns** → add-ons rail → fine-print row (service radius, condition surcharge, policies).

- **Pricing display:** "starting at" + size columns + one-line condition disclaimer is the industry compromise. Mobile Tech RX argues condition-based pricing makes ~40% more than fixed menus; hiding prices entirely is a listed flyer mistake. This *perfectly* fits Carolina Gloss's quote-first funnel: menu shows "from $X," form locks the exact price.
- **Steering:** "Most Popular" badge on the middle tier. Ceramic/correction broken out separately (different price universe). Maintenance/subscription plans as their own block — and note reference #04: the subscription post was the engagement winner.
- **Package naming families:** metals (Bronze/Silver/Gold), speed/depth (Express/Full/Showroom), branded (Panda "Black"), levels. QuoteIQ's counterpoint: plain names convert because they're instantly understood. **Hybrid opportunity: a "Gloss" family** — e.g., Express Gloss / Full Gloss / Showroom Gloss — plain descriptor + ownable brand word.
- **Complete menus also carry:** "we come to you" stated explicitly, named service cities, per-package duration, what's-included checklists, QR to booking, deposit/cancellation line (25% deposit is a seen norm), personal-items fine print, insured badge, IG handle, payment methods.
- **Typography:** one bold condensed caps display face + one clean sans, hierarchy via size/spacing/weight — never chrome bevels or glows. Prices big; inclusion lists small and light (restraint there is what makes prices pop).
- **Premium color code:** near-black/charcoal ground, restrained accent, generous negative space, gloss lives in the *photography* not in text effects. Cheap signals: neon brights, discount-first headlines, clip-art, everything-bold.
- **Formats:** IG feed 1080×1350 (4:5) or the new uncropped 1080×1440 (3:4 — strong candidate for menu posts since the grid now previews 3:4); story 1080×1920; Google Business 1200×900 with 900×900 safe center; print at 300 DPI with 3mm bleed — flyerease specifically recommends **4×9" door hangers** for residential mobile-detailing canvassing.

## 5. Instagram content system (grid / stories / highlights / carousels)

**Grid post types, ranked by consensus:** 1) before/after transformations — interiors are the most *shareable* (parents/pet owners tag friends) and neglected daily drivers outperform supercars; 2) close-up gloss/reflection shots (the grid's aesthetic backbone); 3) process/action shots incl. the mobile rig; 4) minimal review graphics — **screenshots of real Google reviews beat designed testimonial graphics**; 5) educational carousels (save magnets); 6) price/package posts framed as outcomes ("Your paint deserves lifetime protection") not menus.

**Before/after credibility protocol** (this becomes a client SOP): shoot befores from 3–4 fixed positions *before touching the car*; identical angle, distance, lighting (overcast is ideal — "the sky is one giant softbox"); lock exposure so the after isn't just brighter; shoot the after **dry**; label simply BEFORE/AFTER; post as 2–3-slide carousel (split image + full-res receipts); caption with vehicle, condition, hours, service ("3 years of pet hair, 4.5 hours" beats "amazing transformation!").

**Stories:** daily cadence even when feed is 3–5×/week. Booking link **sticker renamed** ("Book now" / "2 slots left"); DM-keyword + sticker combo; slots-left scarcity tied to local context ("3 Charlotte slots left this week"); polls/sliders/question box (harvest FAQ carousel material); countdown stickers on offer deadlines; Google-review screenshot reshares → archived into Reviews highlight.

**Highlights:** 5–6 max, one word each, icon centered in the ~500px safe circle, one consistent system. Canonical detailer row: **Book · Before/After · Reviews · Pricing · Ceramic · FAQ** (+ seasonal "Pollen SZN" as optional 7th). White line-icons on Carolina-blue circles is exactly the prescribed pattern — and a natural home for the crest logo elements. Keep highlights stocked; a stale Reviews highlight is anti-proof.

**Carousels:** 4:5 throughout, never mixed ratios; hook slide owns 80% of the outcome (specific claim in 8–10 words, logo ≤15% of canvas); one idea per slide, ≤25 words; 7–10 slides for education, 2–3 for reveals; CTA slide mirrors the hook slide's design; "Comment GLOSS for a quote" keyword capture. FAQ carousels and "what's included" package breakdowns (one included item per slide, shown being done on a real car) are the detailing-specific formats.

**Cadence & grid:** 3–5 feed posts/week + daily stories; batch-shoot 10–15 pieces on one busy day → 2–3 weeks of content. **Ratio: ~70–80% real photos / 20–30% designed graphics** (matches the 70/30 value-to-promo rule). Cohesion comes from one photo edit (cool-neutral, slightly lifted blues) + a small consistent Carolina-blue label device — not from templating every post. All-template grids pattern-match to franchise/spam accounts and carry zero proof.

## 6. Paid static ads (Meta) — what converts for local detailing

- Statics still drive ~60–70% of Meta conversions and beat video for simple offers. **Winning stack:** real before/after + price-anchored first-time offer + city callout + stars-with-count strip + one CTA.
- **Format ranking:** before/after splits ("2018 BMW 3-Series — 6-Hour Paint Correction"); offer-led ("Interior Special — First-Time $99, reg. $149, 10 spots this week"); review/proof statics; package carousels. Convenience ("we come to you") is a mandatory supporting line, not the headline.
- **Hooks:** local callout ("Charlotte car owners—"), disgust-factor interiors ("When's the last time you looked under the kids' car seats?"), outcome language ("showroom fresh," "mirror finish"), seasonal ("Protect your paint before pollen season"), segment callouts ("Tesla owners:").
- **Offers — Rule of 100:** under $100 use % off, over $100 use $ off. Always anchor ("Reg. $149 → $99"). Free add-on preserves price integrity better than discounting. Credible scarcity = limited weekly slots (natural for a solo mobile operator).
- **Specs:** 1080×1350 + 1080×1080 + 1080×1920 set; 9:16 safe zones (top ~14%, bottom ~20–35% clear); one headline + one price + one CTA on-image (heavy text throttles delivery); sweet spot is *real driveway photo with a clean designed overlay strip* — not a full poster, not raw lo-fi.
- **Proof:** "4.9 ★ · 200+ Google reviews" format (count matters: 59% only trust 20+ reviews); city names on-creative; 15-mile radius targeting norm.
- **Flow:** ad price must match the booking page (message match). Lead forms are ~20–30% cheaper per lead but need fast follow-up. Specialist booking tools: Urable, OrbisX (vehicle-size pricing, deposits, upsells).
- **Economics:** $20–50/day start; $25–50 CPL benchmark (documented detailing campaign at $14.96); leads in 7–14 days; 3–5 variants, one new creative/week; never judge before ~7 days / 25–50 events.

## 7. Premium craft — the anti-"AI slop" playbook

**Why it matters commercially:** ~63% of consumers are less likely to buy from brands using AI ads; 73% distrust ads they *suspect* are AI; the penalty is for **detectability, not AI use** (Bynder: 56% preferred unlabeled AI copy until told). Cars are AI's hardest subject: coherent paint reflections + brand-exact geometry + badge text all fail together — and **reflections are literally this client's product**.

**AI-tells checklist (kill any asset showing one):**
- Melted/warped badges or logos (the #1 tell) · garbled plate/sign text · mismatched wheel-spoke counts, ovalized rims
- Paint reflecting a scene that doesn't exist, or two panels reflecting different worlds
- Missing tire contact shadow ("floating car" — the single most important believability element), light direction contradicting shadows, color-temp mismatch between subject and background, edge halos
- Plastic/waxy texture on everything; fake uniform bokeh; the generic "epic moody garage" neon look; crushed blacks + HDR oversaturation
- Chunky slightly-melted 3D chrome lettering; mystery sparkles/debris filling every corner; meaningless badges/shields; multiple competing fonts; zero negative space

**Safe-use ladder:** ① real photo + pro retouch (hero) → ② real photo + AI cleanup (remove bins, fix sky) → ③ real car cutout + AI *quiet* background, graded as one image → ④ AI atmosphere backplates behind typography (no car) → ⑤ ~~fully AI car beauty shots~~ **never**.

**De-AI-ing finishing pass** for anything AI-touched: 2–4% monochromatic grain, desaturate 10–15%, lift blacks slightly, imperfect crop, one unifying curves/LUT pass, painted-in contact shadow.

**Photography looks to mimic (all garage-achievable):** golden-hour raking light at f/8–f/11; the polarizer look (kill ugly reflections, *keep* the clean specular streak — over-polarizing makes gloss look matte); low-angle front/rear ¾ hero angle; backlit water-beading macro (the ceramic proof shot); dark-studio **light painting** with a $50 LED tube — a premium look AI can't fake, so it doubles as an authenticity signal.

**Design system:** 2–3 colors (near-black ground, Carolina blue accent, white type); 2 fonts max. Font system (all free/Google): **Anton or Barlow Condensed Bold** (impact headlines, caps, +5–15% tracking) + **Archivo/Space Grotesk** (wide premium caps for "CERAMIC COATING" layer) + **Inter or Montserrat** (body). Luxury-auto code: ~80% of luxury car brands run all-caps spaced logotypes; condensed = motorsport energy, wide-spaced = calm premium — blend both. Avoid Orbitron-class sci-fi faces. Generous negative space ("we don't need to overwhelm you to impress you"); subtle grain/carbon texture at low opacity instead of bevels/glows/rainbow gradients; never stretch type or logos.

**Template architecture (the "swappable variables" system):** central kit (color tokens, type styles, logo lockups) → 6–8 locked masters (before/after split, single hero, 3-photo process, offer/price, review card, story CTA) × formats (4:5, 1:1, 9:16, flyer) → **photo zones as smart-object/placeholder slots** with the grain + grade layers sitting *above* the slot so every swapped real photo inherits the house look. In Canva: Brand Templates with element locks (team can only swap photos/copy). The locked system is itself the anti-slop insurance — AI never touches layout or type.

**Print/QR:** 300 DPI, 3mm bleed, rich black (not 100K), CMYK-checked accents; QR ≥2cm handheld / ≥5cm poster, 4-module quiet zone, dark-on-light, framed into the grid with "Scan to book" caption (branded codes get up to ~40% more scans); print a physical proof and scan-test it. Reference #06 shows QR-forward signage working as *content* too.
